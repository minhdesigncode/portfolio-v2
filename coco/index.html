Case study · AI governance · Australia

Keeping AI agents in check

Designing the MVP of Coco, a governance gateway that checks every action an AI agent takes against company policy, before it runs. From a technical draft flow to a product risk teams can actually use.

Client
Coco AI, Melbourne
Role
Product Designer
Team
Founders, engineers and a designer-engineer in Australia
Status
MVP live, full flow
01 · Context

AI agents are starting to move money. Who checks them first?

Companies are handing more work to AI agents, and some of that work touches cash directly: paying vendors, approving applicants, closing fraud alerts. One wrong action can be expensive and hard to reverse.

Coco sits between the agent and the action. A company turns its rules, culture and policies into behavioural contracts. Before an agent acts, Coco checks the action against those contracts and returns a verdict. Every verdict is written to an audit log the agent cannot touch. The result: teams can let agents do more, with less risk and less human overhead.

Auto-execute

Within the rules. The agent goes ahead.

Observe

Allowed, but flagged so patterns can be watched.

Escalate

Held until a human approves or rejects.

Block

Breaks a rule. Stopped, with the reason recorded.

02 · The brief

A smart idea, a draft flow, and a lot of jargon

The founders came with a clear idea and a draft flow. My job was to turn it into a real product: set up the design system, sit with the team to understand what every module actually does, and shape screens around it.

The tricky part: Coco is deeply technical, but its users are not only engineers. Risk and compliance teams, who may never open a terminal, need to review escalations, read audit trails and trust the verdicts.

Technical depth

Contracts, verdicts, SDKs

Every concept needed plain-language naming and a clear home.

Two audiences

Engineers and risk teams

Setup is for developers; daily review is for people who are not.

MVP, full flow

Small but complete

From sign-up to the first verdict, nothing could be a dead end.

04 · Onboarding

Six steps to a working agent

Sign in, create the organization, name a project and pick its workflow family (payments, KYC, AML and fraud, or custom). Choose the framework the agent runs on, from Claude Code and Cursor to LangGraph and n8n, and Coco generates the right SDK snippet. Pick a plan, copy the API key, and once the agent is wired up in the terminal, skip straight to the dashboard.

05 · Dashboard

Numbers start moving the moment the agent runs

The first screen after setup. Verdict totals use the same four colors everywhere, so a spike in red reads instantly. Recent alerts surface what needs attention, and every project sits below with its contracts, verdicts and escalations.

06 · Escalate & audit

Humans stay in the loop

Escalations are actions held for human review before the agent may proceed, like a $128,000 payment or approving a tier 3 applicant. Each card shows the contract and the exact action, with Approve and Reject side by side and an SLA badge when review runs late.

Audit logs record every verdict. They are immutable and append-only; the agent cannot write there. Open any row to see what happened in plain English, which checks passed, and the contract behind it, with a developer view folded away underneath.

07 · Contracts & evaluation

Turning policy documents into rules agents follow

Contracts are the heart of Coco. A bank or company uploads its policies (tax rules, compliance guidelines, do's and don'ts) and Coco's AI drafts behavioural contracts from each clause, like skills for an agent. Every contract cites its source, and a human reviews it before it goes live.

Evaluation closes the loop. An LLM scores each agent while people rate individual verdicts from Critical to Excellent. Together they suggest where contracts are too tight or too loose, so the rules and the agents improve over time.

08 · Configure

Controls, keys and team

Controls define the metrics used to judge an agent: which step they watch (tool or LLM), when they run (pre or post) and where (server or SDK). SDK & API keys manage how Coco connects to each environment. Settings covers members and roles, usage costs, billing and two-factor authentication.

09 · Design system

One system for brand, product and code

I built Coco's design system with a product designer and software engineer in Australia, so design and code speak the same language. It starts from the brand guideline (logo, a violet core on warm paper, Cal Sans type) and carries through to product tokens in light and dark.

The verdict colors became first-class semantic tokens: Auto-execute, Observe, Escalate and Deny, each with 60 and 10 tints, plus Documentation and Integration. Color names are unique and playful (a Dragon Ball Z naming scheme) so they stay easy to remember across products.

10 · Team

Built across Vietnam and Melbourne

A distributed team, with members in Vietnam and Melbourne, working fully in English and meeting weekly with the founders and engineers to land on final solutions.
